## What Is Limb Lengthening Surgery?

Limb lengthening surgery uses the body's ability to create new bone after a controlled surgical separation of the bone.

The process begins with an **osteotomy**, in which the surgeon carefully cuts the bone while preserving as much of its blood supply and surrounding soft tissue as possible. A fixation device is then used to maintain alignment.

After an initial healing period, the two bone segments are gradually moved apart. This creates a controlled gap in which new bone, called **regenerate bone**, develops.

The gradual process is called **distraction osteogenesis**. It is fundamentally different from simply stretching or extending a bone during surgery.

The rate and amount of distraction are individualised. The surgeon monitors X-rays and the patient's clinical progress and may adjust treatment according to the quality of new bone formation and the condition of the surrounding muscles, tendons, nerves and joints.

## Why Is Limb Lengthening Surgery Performed?

Limb lengthening is not one single operation for one single condition. The underlying reason for treatment determines the surgical plan.

### Leg-length discrepancy

A significant difference between the lengths of the two legs can affect walking mechanics and body alignment.

Depending on the cause and magnitude of the discrepancy, treatment may involve observation, shoe modification, growth modulation in children, shortening of the longer limb, or lengthening of the shorter limb.

Limb lengthening can be considered when increasing the length of the shorter limb is appropriate. The choice depends on age, skeletal maturity, discrepancy, deformity and overall limb function.

### Congenital limb shortening

Some people are born with one limb shorter than the other or with abnormalities affecting bone development.

Limb reconstruction may involve staged lengthening, deformity correction, or a combination of procedures. The treatment plan is usually individualised over several stages rather than treated as a single operation. When the honest product is rebuilding a deficient segment rather than isolated distraction, the neighbouring sheet is [limb reconstruction surgery](/costs/India/Pediatric-Orthopaedic/Limb-Reconstruction-Surgery).

### Post-traumatic limb shortening

Fractures, bone loss, infection or previous orthopaedic surgery can leave a limb shorter than the opposite side.

In appropriately selected patients, gradual lengthening can restore part of the lost length while also addressing associated deformity. Modern internal lengthening nails have been used for selected post-traumatic cases, although anatomy, bone quality, infection status and deformity can limit their use. Adult trauma lists that are not yet a named lengthening nail may still sit on [non-union repair](/costs/India/Orthopedics/Non-Union-Repair) or [fracture fixation](/costs/India/Orthopedics/Fracture-Fixation) until films decide.

### Skeletal dysplasia

Some skeletal dysplasias are associated with short stature and disproportion between different parts of the body.

Lengthening may form part of a broader reconstructive strategy in selected patients. The timing and extent of treatment require specialised paediatric or adult limb-reconstruction expertise.

### Bone reconstruction

Distraction osteogenesis may also be used in complex reconstructive situations involving bone defects or shortening.

These cases are generally more complicated than elective stature lengthening because the surgeon may simultaneously need to manage deformity, previous infection, soft-tissue problems or nonunion.

### Limb deformity correction

Shortening may occur together with angular or rotational deformity.

In selected patients, the surgeon can combine lengthening with correction of alignment. The objective is not simply to make the bone longer but to restore useful mechanical alignment and function. Combined paediatric osteotomy work may sit on [pediatric deformity correction](/costs/India/Pediatric-Orthopaedic/Pediatric-Deformity-Correction).

### Cosmetic stature lengthening

Cosmetic stature lengthening is performed to increase height in carefully selected adults.

Published studies have reported meaningful height gains and high patient satisfaction in selected groups, but the evidence is largely based on case series and retrospective studies. Complications involving joints, muscles, nerves, bone healing, infection and fixation devices remain important considerations.

Cosmetic height lengthening should therefore be approached as major orthopaedic surgery rather than as a simple cosmetic procedure. GAF does not publish a single adult stature-lengthening tariff.

## Limb Lengthening for Medical Reasons vs Cosmetic Height Increase

These two applications should not be confused.

| Medical limb lengthening | Cosmetic stature lengthening |
| --- | --- |
| Treats a structural or functional orthopaedic problem | Intended primarily to increase height |
| May correct leg-length discrepancy | Usually performed on both lower limbs |
| Can address post-traumatic shortening | Generally performed in skeletally mature adults |
| May be combined with deformity correction | Requires careful assessment of expectations |
| Treatment benefits are primarily functional or reconstructive | Benefits are primarily related to stature and body image |
| Medical indication guides the procedure | Patient selection and informed consent are particularly important |

A 2020 systematic review of cosmetic stature lengthening involving 795 patients reported a mean final lengthening of approximately 6.7 cm across the included studies, although techniques and patient populations varied substantially. The authors also emphasised that the available evidence was heterogeneous and that clear indications and guidelines remain important.

A more recent systematic review covering studies through July 2024 reported average lengthening of approximately 67 mm across included aesthetic-limb-lengthening studies, while also documenting complications involving joints, tendons, infection, bone healing, hardware and neurovascular structures.

These figures should **not** be interpreted as a guaranteed result for an individual patient.

## How Does Limb Lengthening Surgery Work?

The procedure generally follows several stages.

### Stage 1: Detailed assessment

The surgeon evaluates current limb length, amount of discrepancy, femoral and tibial proportions, bone alignment, rotation, joint movement, muscle and tendon flexibility, bone quality, previous surgeries, infection history, nerve and blood-vessel status, and general medical condition.

Full-length standing X-rays and other imaging studies may be required. For cosmetic stature lengthening, the surgeon also needs to assess the proportions of the entire body rather than considering height alone.

### Stage 2: Osteotomy

The surgeon creates a controlled surgical break in the selected bone.

The goal is to preserve biological conditions that allow new bone to develop. The bone is then stabilised using an appropriate fixation system.

### Stage 3: Latency period

The patient generally has a short period after surgery before active distraction begins.

This allows early healing and formation of the biological environment required for gradual bone regeneration. The exact timing varies according to the surgical technique and individual patient.

### Stage 4: Distraction phase

The two bone segments are gradually separated.

With appropriate distraction, new bone forms between the segments. This gradual process allows the bone and surrounding soft tissues to adapt over time. Continuous or gradual lengthening creates substantially less acute soft-tissue stress than immediately lengthening the bone during surgery.

### Stage 5: Consolidation

Once the desired length has been achieved, the bone must mature and become strong enough to withstand normal loads.

This phase is called **consolidation**. The fixation device may remain in place during this period depending on the technique used.

### Stage 6: Rehabilitation

Physiotherapy is a central component of limb lengthening.

Treatment may focus on maintaining joint range of motion, preserving muscle flexibility, maintaining strength, preventing contractures, improving gait, gradually increasing weight-bearing and returning to daily activities.

The rehabilitation programme should be designed around the patient's specific bone, fixation method and progress.

## Types of Limb Lengthening Techniques

Two broad approaches are commonly used.

### Internal limb lengthening nail

An internal lengthening nail is placed inside the bone.

Modern motorised nails can gradually extend the bone from within. Some systems use a remote-control mechanism to activate the telescopic nail.

One advantage is that there is no large external frame surrounding the limb.

Hospital for Special Surgery describes internal lengthening nails and external fixation as the two principal ways of gradually lengthening bone after osteotomy.

Internal nails, however, are not suitable for every patient. The diameter and length of the bone, skeletal maturity, anatomy, deformity, bone quality and other clinical factors can influence whether an intramedullary nail is appropriate.

![Adult anatomical femur specimen with a telescopic internal lengthening nail in the canal at a midshaft osteotomy](/uploads/treatments/limb-lengthening-nail.webp)

### External fixator limb lengthening

An external fixator is a frame outside the limb.

Pins, wires or screws connect the frame to the bone. The frame can be adjusted gradually to separate the bone segments and, when necessary, correct alignment.

External fixation remains particularly useful in complex deformity correction, certain paediatric cases, small bones, significant deformities, and situations where an internal nail is not appropriate.

The main disadvantage is the presence of the external frame and the need for meticulous pin-site care. Pin-site infection is one of the most commonly reported problems associated with external fixation.

![Adult anatomical lower-leg specimen with a circular external ring fixator applied to the tibia for gradual lengthening](/uploads/treatments/limb-lengthening-frame.webp)

| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Inside the bone | Outside the limb |

There is no universally best technique for every patient. The surgeon should select the method according to the clinical objective rather than choosing a device simply because it is newer.

## How Much Height Can Be Gained With Limb Lengthening?

There is no single safe or appropriate amount of lengthening for every patient.

The achievable amount depends on femur or tibia, original bone length, soft-tissue flexibility, muscle and tendon tolerance, joint movement, bone quality, patient age, previous surgery, overall body proportions, fixation method and rate of bone formation.

Research in cosmetic stature lengthening has reported average gains around 6–7 cm in several systematic reviews, but individual results vary considerably.

More length is not automatically better. As the bone becomes longer, muscles, tendons, nerves, blood vessels, skin and joints must accommodate the change. Excessive lengthening can increase the risk of soft-tissue problems and functional limitations.

A surgeon should therefore establish a realistic target rather than promising a particular number of centimetres.

## Femur vs Tibia Lengthening

Both the femur and tibia can be lengthened.

### Femur lengthening

The femur is the thigh bone. Femoral lengthening may be performed for limb-length discrepancy, reconstruction, deformity correction or cosmetic stature lengthening. Internal nails are commonly considered in appropriately selected adult patients.

### Tibia lengthening

The tibia is the main weight-bearing bone of the lower leg. Tibial lengthening can be technically demanding because the ankle and surrounding soft tissues must accommodate the gradual increase in length. Ankle stiffness and equinus deformity are recognised concerns during lower-limb lengthening.

### Femur and tibia lengthening

In selected cosmetic patients, femoral and tibial lengthening may be performed as separate stages. Doing both segments is substantially more demanding than treating a single bone. The decision depends on the patient's anatomy, treatment goals, the surgeon's assessment, rehabilitation capacity and risk tolerance.

## Who Is a Candidate for Limb Lengthening Surgery?

Candidate selection is highly individualised.

For medically indicated lengthening, appropriate candidates may include patients with significant leg-length discrepancy, congenital limb shortening, post-traumatic shortening, selected skeletal dysplasias, bone defects, certain deformities associated with shortening, and selected reconstructive problems.

For cosmetic stature lengthening, the assessment is more selective. The patient should generally have reached skeletal maturity, have appropriate bone anatomy, understand the prolonged rehabilitation process, and have realistic expectations. The surgeon should also determine whether the desired outcome can be achieved without creating an unacceptable risk to function.

## Who May Not Be Suitable for Limb Lengthening?

Limb lengthening may be inappropriate or require modification in patients with certain conditions.

Potential concerns include active bone or soft-tissue infection, poor bone quality, significant uncontrolled medical disease, severe vascular problems, inadequate soft-tissue condition, certain nerve disorders, open growth plates when an adult-type internal nail is being considered, inability to participate in prolonged rehabilitation, unrealistic expectations, and anatomical limitations that make the proposed technique unsuitable.

The exact contraindications depend on the indication and the device being considered. For example, substantial deformity, narrow intramedullary canals and active infection can limit the use of internal lengthening nails.

## Evaluation Before Limb Lengthening Surgery

A comprehensive preoperative evaluation may include:

**Clinical examination.** The surgeon assesses limb alignment, joint movement, muscle tightness, gait, strength, scars, previous surgical sites and neurovascular status.

**Limb-length measurement.** Accurate measurement is essential because apparent shortening can sometimes be caused by pelvic tilt, joint deformity or other alignment problems.

**Full-length X-rays.** Standing long-leg radiographs may be used to evaluate overall alignment and limb proportions.

**CT or MRI.** Additional imaging may be required for complex deformities, previous surgery, joint pathology or other specific questions.

**Blood tests.** Routine preoperative investigations may be required depending on age and medical history.

**Infection assessment.** Patients with previous fractures, implants or bone infection may need additional investigation before reconstruction.

**Physiotherapy assessment.** Preoperative flexibility can influence the ability of muscles and joints to tolerate gradual lengthening.

## Limb Lengthening Surgery Procedure

The exact operation differs from patient to patient, but a typical treatment pathway may look like this:

1. **Preoperative planning.** The surgeon calculates the desired correction and determines the appropriate bone segment and fixation method.
2. **Anaesthesia.** The operation is performed under appropriate anaesthesia, commonly general or regional anaesthesia depending on the case.
3. **Osteotomy.** The selected bone is carefully divided.
4. **Fixation.** An internal lengthening nail or external fixation system is applied.
5. **Initial recovery.** The patient is monitored for pain, swelling, circulation, nerve function and other immediate postoperative issues.
6. **Distraction.** Gradual lengthening begins according to the surgeon's protocol.
7. **X-ray monitoring.** Regular imaging is used to evaluate alignment and regenerate bone formation.
8. **Physiotherapy.** Joint movement and muscle flexibility are maintained throughout treatment.
9. **Consolidation.** The newly formed bone strengthens.
10. **Return to normal activity.** Weight-bearing and activity are gradually increased according to bone healing and clinical recovery.

## Physiotherapy After Limb Lengthening

Physiotherapy is not optional simply because the bone is being lengthened successfully. The surrounding soft tissues are also adapting.

A rehabilitation programme may include knee range-of-motion exercises, hip mobility, ankle dorsiflexion, hamstring stretching, quadriceps strengthening, calf stretching, core strengthening, gait training, gradual weight-bearing and functional rehabilitation.

The precise exercises should be prescribed by the treating orthopaedic and physiotherapy team. Failure to maintain joint movement can contribute to stiffness and contractures.

## Pain During Limb Lengthening

Pain varies between patients.

The surgical procedure itself causes postoperative pain, while the subsequent distraction phase can produce discomfort as the bone and soft tissues adapt.

Pain may be influenced by rate of distraction, amount of lengthening, bone segment, muscle tightness, nerve irritation, fixation method and individual pain sensitivity.

Severe, increasing or unusual pain should not simply be considered part of normal recovery.

New severe pain, numbness, weakness, swelling, fever, wound or pin-site drainage, sudden chest pain, breathlessness, or a swollen painful calf should be assessed in a **local emergency department**, not by WhatsApp. Those symptoms can indicate nerve stretch, deep infection, pulmonary embolism or DVT.

## Risks and Complications of Limb Lengthening Surgery

Limb lengthening is a complex procedure with recognised risks.

**Infection.** External fixation carries a risk of pin-site infection. More serious infections can involve deeper tissues or bone.

**Joint stiffness.** The knee, ankle or hip may become stiff during treatment.

**Muscle contracture.** Muscles and tendons may become progressively tight as the bone lengthens.

**Nerve irritation or injury.** Nerves can be affected by stretching or compression during lengthening.

**Blood-vessel complications.** Neurovascular structures must be monitored carefully because gradual lengthening places stress on surrounding tissues.

**Delayed bone healing.** The regenerate may develop more slowly than expected.

**Premature consolidation.** Bone can become sufficiently mature before the desired amount of lengthening is completed, potentially requiring adjustment or additional treatment.

**Nonunion.** The bone may fail to unite adequately and may require further intervention.

**Malalignment.** The bone may gradually develop angular or rotational deformity during treatment.

**Regenerate deformity.** Newly formed bone can deform if it is not adequately supported while it matures.

**Hardware problems.** Internal nails, screws or external fixation components can fail, migrate, bend or require revision.

**Blood clots.** As with other major orthopaedic procedures, venous thromboembolism is a potential risk.

**Additional surgery.** Some patients require unplanned procedures to address complications or problems during treatment.

A systematic review and meta-analysis of cosmetic limb lengthening emphasises that both external fixation and internal nailing can produce complications, even though patient satisfaction is often reported as high in published series.

## Is Limb Lengthening Surgery Safe?

Limb lengthening can be performed successfully in appropriately selected patients by experienced orthopaedic teams, but it should not be described as risk-free.

The level of risk depends on the reason for surgery, amount of lengthening, surgical method, patient health, bone quality, previous operations and rehabilitation.

A 2020 systematic review of cosmetic stature lengthening found favourable functional outcomes and satisfaction in published studies but also identified problems such as ankle equinus, pin-site infection, regenerate deformity, and subtalar stiffness or deformity.

More recent evidence continues to report complications involving infection, joint and tendon problems, bone healing, hardware and neurovascular structures.

The appropriate question is therefore not simply "Is limb lengthening safe?"

A more useful question is: "Is limb lengthening appropriate for this patient, and what are the expected benefits, risks, alternatives and rehabilitation requirements?"

There is no universal stay duration.

## Can Patients Walk After Limb Lengthening Surgery?

Walking restrictions depend on the surgical technique and surgeon's protocol.

Some internal lengthening nails permit controlled weight-bearing, while other situations require more protection. External fixation protocols also vary.

Hospital for Special Surgery notes that many patients progress to walking without crutches after a period of recovery, but the timing varies according to the amount of lengthening and individual healing.

Patients should never increase weight-bearing simply because pain has decreased. The bone must be sufficiently strong, and the treating surgeon should determine when progression is appropriate.

## Can Limb Lengthening Affect Normal Walking?

The objective of reconstructive limb lengthening is to achieve a functional limb.

However, temporary gait changes are common during treatment because of pain, weakness, restricted joint movement, external frames or weight-bearing restrictions. Contractures and joint stiffness can also affect function.

Published research has identified joint and tendon complications as important issues in aesthetic limb lengthening. Appropriate patient selection and consistent physiotherapy are therefore important.

## Does Limb Lengthening Leave Scars?

Yes.

The size and location of scars depend on the surgical approach and fixation method. Internal lengthening nails generally require surgical incisions for insertion and locking screws. External fixation involves multiple pin or wire sites and can leave several small scars. Previous surgery, infection, wound problems and additional procedures may increase scarring.

## Can the Lengthening Nail Be Removed?

In some patients, the internal lengthening nail is later removed after the bone has adequately consolidated.

Whether removal is necessary depends on the implant, patient, symptoms, surgeon's preference and clinical circumstances. Implant removal is itself another surgical procedure and should therefore be discussed as part of the initial treatment plan and quotation.

## How Long Does the New Bone Take to Heal?

Bone healing varies between individuals.

The treatment has two major biological periods. **Distraction:** the bone segments are gradually separated. **Consolidation:** the newly formed bone becomes stronger and more mature.

The amount of lengthening, bone involved, patient health, nutrition, smoking status, mechanical stability and individual biology can influence healing. External fixation studies have historically measured treatment duration in relation to centimetres of lengthening, but these figures vary considerably between patients and techniques.

## Nutrition During Limb Lengthening

Good nutrition supports recovery. Patients should maintain adequate intake of protein, calcium, vitamin D, iron when deficient, and other essential micronutrients.

The specific supplementation plan should be based on the patient's nutritional status and treating physician's advice.

Smoking is an important concern because tobacco exposure can adversely affect bone healing. Patients should discuss smoking and nicotine use with their orthopaedic team before surgery.

## Psychological Considerations in Cosmetic Stature Lengthening

Cosmetic stature lengthening requires careful consideration of expectations.

The surgery can involve months of physical rehabilitation, restricted mobility, discomfort, repeated imaging and uncertainty about the final recovery.

Patients should understand that the procedure is major surgery, height gain is not guaranteed to a specific number, complications can require additional surgery, recovery can be prolonged, the result may not perfectly match expectations, and psychological support may be useful for some patients.

Published literature reports improvements in body image and satisfaction in many selected patients, but the evidence is heterogeneous and does not mean that every patient will experience the same psychological outcome.

## Alternatives to Limb Lengthening Surgery

Limb lengthening is not always the first treatment.

Depending on the condition, alternatives may include observation, shoe lifts, orthotic devices, physiotherapy, growth modulation in children, epiphysiodesis in selected growing patients, shortening of the longer limb, acute deformity correction and other reconstructive procedures.

The appropriate option depends on the patient's age, discrepancy, skeletal maturity, symptoms, anatomy and treatment objectives.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Limb Lengthening Surgery in India

### Is limb lengthening surgery available in India?

Yes. Limb lengthening and reconstruction procedures are performed in specialised orthopaedic centres in India using external fixation and, in appropriately selected patients, internal lengthening nails.

### Is limb lengthening surgery painful?

Pain and discomfort are possible after surgery and during the lengthening process. Pain management and physiotherapy form important parts of postoperative care.

### How many centimetres can limb lengthening achieve?

There is no universal safe number. Published cosmetic-lengthening studies have reported average gains of approximately 6–7 cm, but individual outcomes vary and larger lengthening can increase soft-tissue and functional risks.

### Can I increase my height through limb lengthening?

Selected skeletally mature adults can undergo cosmetic stature lengthening. However, the procedure is major orthopaedic surgery and requires prolonged rehabilitation and careful assessment of risks and expectations.

### Is internal lengthening better than an external fixator?

Neither technique is universally better. Internal nails can reduce some problems associated with external fixation, while external fixation remains valuable for complex deformities, certain paediatric cases, and situations where an internal nail is unsuitable.

### How long does limb lengthening take?

The total treatment period depends on the amount of lengthening, bone healing, fixation method, rehabilitation and complications. The treatment includes both distraction and consolidation phases.

### Can both legs be lengthened?

Yes, bilateral lower-limb lengthening may be performed in selected cases, particularly in cosmetic stature lengthening. The treatment is considerably more demanding than lengthening one limb.

### Is limb lengthening permanent?

Once the newly formed bone has completely consolidated, the achieved bone length is intended to be permanent. However, the final functional result depends on bone healing, alignment, soft-tissue adaptation and rehabilitation.

### Can limb lengthening correct a leg-length discrepancy?

Yes. Gradual lengthening is an established method for treating selected leg-length discrepancies. The exact treatment depends on the cause and size of the discrepancy.

### Can limb lengthening correct deformity?

In selected patients, lengthening can be combined with correction of angular or rotational deformity.

### Is limb lengthening safe for children?

Children require a different approach because their growth plates may still be open. The technique and timing must be determined by a paediatric orthopaedic or limb-reconstruction specialist. GAF paediatric planning is **$12,000–$28,000**.

### Can I return to sports after limb lengthening?

Return to sports depends on complete bone healing, joint movement, muscle strength, gait and the surgeon's assessment. High-impact activity should not be resumed simply because the distraction phase has finished.

### Does cosmetic limb lengthening affect walking?

The objective is to preserve function, but temporary gait changes and complications such as joint stiffness or contracture can occur. Appropriate rehabilitation is essential.

### Does limb lengthening cause nerve problems?

Nerve irritation or injury is a recognised potential complication because nerves must adapt to gradual changes in limb length. New numbness, weakness or severe pain should be reported immediately. Sudden loss of circulation or rapidly worsening neurological change belongs in a local emergency department.

### Can limb lengthening cause blood clots?

Major orthopaedic surgery and prolonged reduced mobility can increase the risk of venous thromboembolism. The treating team assesses individual risk and determines whether preventive measures are required. Chest pain, breathlessness or a swollen painful calf is an emergency-department problem, not a WhatsApp question.

### Will I need physiotherapy?

Yes. Physiotherapy is an important part of limb lengthening because maintaining joint movement and muscle flexibility helps reduce functional problems during treatment.

## Key Benefits of Limb Lengthening Surgery

Depending on the indication, potential benefits may include correction of leg-length discrepancy, improved limb symmetry, restoration of limb length after trauma, correction of selected deformities, reconstruction of certain bone defects, improved limb proportions, and increased height in selected cosmetic patients.

The expected benefit depends entirely on the underlying condition and treatment goal.

## Important Limitations

Limb lengthening should never be presented as a guaranteed solution.

It may involve multiple months of treatment, repeated X-rays, regular physiotherapy, restricted mobility, temporary dependence on walking aids, additional procedures, complications and long-term follow-up.

The patient's commitment to rehabilitation can be as important as the operation itself.
