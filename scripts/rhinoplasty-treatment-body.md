## What Is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty is surgery that changes the shape or structure of the nose.

The nose is made of bone, cartilage, soft tissue and skin. A rhinoplasty surgeon may reshape or reposition these structures for a functional or aesthetic outcome.

Depending on anatomy and objectives, surgery may reduce a nasal hump, refine the tip, change width, modify the nostrils, straighten a crooked nose, support weak structures or correct a [deviated septum](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty).

The aim is not simply to make the nose smaller. In many cases, successful rhinoplasty is about creating better **facial balance while maintaining or improving nasal function**.

## Why Do People Have Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty may be performed for several different reasons.

### Cosmetic reasons

A patient may be unhappy with a large or small nose, a prominent dorsal hump, a wide nasal bridge, a broad or bulbous nasal tip, a drooping or overly prominent tip, wide or asymmetric nostrils, a crooked or asymmetric nose, an imbalance between the nose and other facial features, or changes after previous nasal trauma.

Rhinoplasty can modify these characteristics while taking overall facial proportions into account.

### Functional reasons

Some patients seek rhinoplasty because they have difficulty breathing through the nose.

The underlying problem may involve a [deviated septum](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty), nasal valve narrowing, structural weakness, previous trauma or other anatomical factors.

When appearance and breathing problems occur together, a surgeon may recommend a **functional rhinoplasty or septorhinoplasty** rather than treating appearance alone.

### Reconstructive reasons

Rhinoplasty may also reconstruct nasal structures affected by trauma, previous surgery, congenital abnormalities, structural collapse, tissue loss or previous unsuccessful rhinoplasty.

Reconstructive surgery can be considerably more complex than a straightforward primary cosmetic rhinoplasty and is quoted after records review.

## What Can Rhinoplasty Change?

Depending on anatomy, rhinoplasty can address several aspects of the nose.

### Nasal bridge

The surgeon may reduce a dorsal hump, straighten the bridge or alter its width.

### Nasal tip

The tip can be refined, reshaped, supported or repositioned depending on the underlying cartilage.

### Nostrils

The size, width or shape of the nostrils can sometimes be modified.

### Overall nasal size

The nose can sometimes be reduced or augmented to create better facial proportions.

### Nasal projection

The relationship between the nasal tip and the rest of the face can be changed.

### Nasal symmetry

Rhinoplasty can improve significant asymmetry, although complete facial or nasal symmetry cannot be guaranteed because natural human faces are not perfectly symmetrical.

### Breathing

When structural obstruction is contributing to breathing difficulty, appropriate nasal surgery can sometimes improve airflow.

## Types of Rhinoplasty

There is no single rhinoplasty technique that is suitable for every patient.

The surgical approach is selected according to anatomy, goals and the changes required.

### 1. Open rhinoplasty

In an open rhinoplasty, the surgeon makes an incision across the columella, the small strip of tissue between the nostrils, in addition to internal nasal incisions.

This allows greater exposure of the underlying nasal framework.

Open rhinoplasty may be particularly useful when detailed structural modification, tip refinement, grafting or revision surgery is required.

A small external scar can occur, although the incision is designed to be discreet.

### 2. Closed rhinoplasty

In closed rhinoplasty, the surgical incisions are made inside the nostrils.

There is no external columellar incision.

Closed rhinoplasty can be suitable for selected patients where the required changes can be performed effectively through an internal approach.

The choice between open and closed rhinoplasty should be based on anatomy and surgical requirements rather than simply the desire to avoid an external incision.

### 3. Functional rhinoplasty

Functional rhinoplasty focuses on improving nasal function, particularly breathing, when structural abnormalities contribute to obstruction.

It may be combined with procedures such as [septoplasty](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty).

### 4. Septorhinoplasty

Septorhinoplasty combines correction of the nasal septum with reshaping or reconstruction of the external nose.

It may be considered when a patient has both a [deviated septum](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty) and external nasal deformity. Combined work beyond a listed primary rhinoplasty is quoted after review.

### 5. Revision rhinoplasty

Revision rhinoplasty is performed after a previous rhinoplasty when further structural, functional or aesthetic correction is required.

Revision surgery can be technically more difficult because previous surgery may have altered the cartilage, bone, soft tissue and available graft material.

Additional cartilage from the ear or rib may sometimes be required in complex cases. There is no separate GAF revision-rhinoplasty sheet; these cases are quoted after records review.

### 6. Reconstructive rhinoplasty

Reconstructive rhinoplasty is performed to restore nasal structure after trauma, congenital abnormalities, previous surgery or tissue loss.

It may require more extensive reconstruction than cosmetic rhinoplasty and is quoted after review.

## Who Is a Good Candidate for Rhinoplasty?

A suitable candidate generally has a specific and realistic understanding of what they would like to change.

Candidates may include people who are dissatisfied with the shape or proportions of their nose, have a nasal hump or broad nasal bridge, have a bulbous, drooping or asymmetric nasal tip, have wide or asymmetric nostrils, have a crooked nose, have breathing difficulties related to nasal structure, have deformity following trauma, require reconstructive nasal surgery, or need revision after previous rhinoplasty.

A surgeon will also consider general health, medications, previous operations, healing history, smoking or vaping, nasal breathing and expectations.

Rhinoplasty is not simply a procedure for producing a particular celebrity or social-media appearance. The surgeon must consider the patient's individual facial proportions and anatomy.

## Rhinoplasty for Men and Women

Rhinoplasty is performed in both men and women.

The surgical plan should be individualized rather than applying the same nasal proportions to every patient.

A surgeon may consider facial proportions, nasal width, nasal projection, tip characteristics, forehead and chin relationship, skin thickness, existing facial asymmetry, breathing function and patient expectations.

The goal is generally a nose that looks appropriate for the individual's face rather than a standardized nose shape.

## Ethnic Rhinoplasty

**Ethnic rhinoplasty** refers to rhinoplasty performed with consideration of the patient's ethnic and anatomical characteristics.

The objective is not to erase ethnic identity.

Instead, the surgeon evaluates the nasal structure, facial proportions, skin and cartilage characteristics and the patient's own goals.

This can be particularly important for patients from South Asia, the Middle East, Africa and other regions where nasal anatomy and aesthetic preferences may differ.

The surgical plan should therefore be personalized rather than based on a generic concept of an “ideal nose.”

## What Happens During Rhinoplasty?

Although every procedure is different, rhinoplasty generally follows several stages.

### 1. Consultation and assessment

The surgeon discusses the patient's goals and medical history.

The nose is examined from the front, side and other angles. Internal nasal examination may also be required, particularly when breathing problems are present.

Photographs are commonly taken to assist with surgical planning.

### 2. Surgical planning

The surgeon determines which structures need to be reduced, reshaped, repositioned or supported.

Depending on the case, the plan may involve bone reshaping, cartilage reshaping, cartilage grafting, tip refinement, dorsal hump reduction, nasal osteotomies, [septoplasty](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty), nasal valve reconstruction, or alar or nostril modification.

### 3. Anaesthesia

Rhinoplasty may be performed under general anaesthesia or, in selected situations, intravenous sedation with local anaesthesia.

The anaesthetic approach depends on the procedure and the patient's circumstances.

### 4. Surgical access

The surgeon uses either an open or closed approach.

### 5. Reshaping

Bone and cartilage are carefully modified according to the surgical plan.

Cartilage grafts may be used when additional structural support is required.

Septal cartilage is commonly used when available. Ear cartilage or rib cartilage may be considered in selected complex cases and is quoted after review.

### 6. Closing and supporting the nose

The skin and soft tissues are repositioned.

Internal splints or an external nasal splint may be used to support healing.

### 7. Recovery

The patient is monitored in a recovery area following surgery.

GAF planning is typically **1–3 nights**. Some hospital protocols use same-day discharge or overnight observation after selected cases. International patients should not plan travel from the shortest published stay.

## How Long Does Rhinoplasty Take?

A straightforward rhinoplasty commonly takes approximately **1.5 to 3 hours**, although surgical duration varies significantly.

Complex revision rhinoplasty, reconstructive procedures, grafting or combined functional surgery can take longer.

The surgeon should provide an estimated duration after assessing anatomy and the planned procedure.

## Rhinoplasty Recovery in India

Recovery after rhinoplasty occurs in stages.

The first week is usually the most noticeable period of swelling, bruising and nasal congestion.

A splint may remain over the nose for approximately one week.

Patients may experience nasal congestion, mild pain or discomfort, swelling, bruising around the eyes, temporary numbness, minor drainage or nosebleeds, and difficulty breathing through the nose initially.

These effects generally improve as healing progresses.

### Typical recovery timeline

| Period | What patients may experience |
| --- | --- |
| First 1–3 days | Swelling, congestion, mild discomfort and drainage |
| Around 1 week | Splint may be removed; early bruising begins improving |
| 1–2 weeks | Many patients can return to routine work depending on recovery |
| 3–4 weeks | Most visible bruising has generally improved |
| 4–6 weeks | Strenuous activity may gradually resume if cleared by the surgeon |
| Several months | Swelling continues to settle and nasal contour becomes clearer |
| Around 1 year or longer | Final refinement may continue, particularly in complex cases |

Recovery varies considerably from patient to patient. The nose can continue changing as swelling resolves, and the final appearance may not be apparent immediately after surgery.

## When Can I Return to Work After Rhinoplasty?

Many patients need approximately **one to two weeks away from work**, particularly if their work is public-facing or physically demanding.

Patients with desk-based work may sometimes return earlier.

The timing depends on swelling, bruising, discomfort, the nature of work and the surgeon's instructions.

International patients should generally allow enough time in India for the initial postoperative review before planning their return journey.

## When Can I Exercise After Rhinoplasty?

Strenuous exercise should generally be avoided during the early recovery period.

Running, weight training, contact sports and activities that could increase blood pressure or expose the nose to trauma may need to be restricted for several weeks.

The surgeon should determine when specific activities can safely resume.

## When Will I See the Final Rhinoplasty Result?

The initial change can be visible soon after surgery, but it is **not the final result**.

Swelling gradually decreases over several months.

Some residual swelling, particularly around the nasal tip, can persist much longer.

Mayo Clinic notes that most swelling may resolve within about a year, while other specialist guidance notes that complete healing can continue beyond that depending on the patient and procedure.

This is why patients should avoid judging the final result during the first few weeks.

## Is Rhinoplasty Painful?

Rhinoplasty is generally associated with discomfort rather than severe pain.

The experience varies between patients and depends on the extent of surgery.

Pain medication prescribed by the surgical team can usually help manage postoperative discomfort.

Congestion and the feeling of pressure may be more noticeable than pain during the first few days.

## Rhinoplasty Risks and Complications

Rhinoplasty is a surgical procedure and does not have a zero-risk outcome.

Potential complications include bleeding, infection, anaesthesia-related complications, persistent swelling, numbness or altered sensation, scarring, nasal asymmetry, difficulty breathing, change in sense of smell, septal perforation, unsatisfactory cosmetic outcome, poor wound healing and the need for revision surgery.

Septal perforation is uncommon but may require additional treatment and may not always be completely correctable.

Patients should discuss individual risk factors with their surgeon before deciding on surgery.

Heavy bleeding, severe pain, fever, breathing difficulty, visual symptoms or rapidly worsening swelling should be assessed in a **local emergency department**.

## Can Rhinoplasty Affect Breathing?

Yes.

This is an important consideration because the nose has both aesthetic and functional roles.

Removing too much structural support or altering the nasal framework without adequately considering airflow can affect breathing.

For patients who already have nasal obstruction, the surgeon should evaluate the internal nasal anatomy before surgery.

When appropriate, rhinoplasty may be combined with [septoplasty](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty) or other functional nasal procedures to address structural obstruction.

For this reason, a good rhinoplasty consultation should not focus only on appearance.

## Rhinoplasty Before Surgery: How to Prepare

Your surgeon will provide personalized instructions.

Depending on your circumstances, preparation may include medical history review, physical examination, nasal examination, photographs, preoperative blood tests or other investigations, medication review, discussion of smoking or vaping, anaesthesia assessment, review of previous nasal surgery and discussion of expectations.

Tell your surgeon about all medicines, supplements and medical conditions.

Do not stop prescribed medicines unless your treating team specifically advises you to do so.

If you smoke or vape, discuss this with your surgeon because nicotine exposure can interfere with healing.

## What to Expect After Rhinoplasty

Immediately after surgery, the nose may be swollen and congested.

A splint may be placed over the nose, and some patients may have internal splints.

You may experience bruising around the eyes.

The surgical team may advise you to keep your head elevated, avoid blowing your nose, sneeze with your mouth open, avoid strenuous exercise, protect the nose from accidental trauma, avoid pressure from spectacles when advised, follow wound-care instructions, take prescribed medicines as directed and attend scheduled follow-up appointments.

These instructions vary according to the exact procedure.

## What Should I Avoid After Rhinoplasty?

Your surgeon may advise avoiding contact sports, heavy exercise, bending or strenuous activity during the early period, blowing the nose, pressure on the nose, unapproved medications or supplements, smoking and vaping, and activities that could cause accidental nasal trauma.

Do not follow generic online recovery instructions in place of your surgeon's postoperative plan.

## Does Rhinoplasty Leave a Scar?

Closed rhinoplasty does not involve an external incision.

Open rhinoplasty involves a small incision across the columella.

The resulting scar is generally designed to be discreet, but scar formation varies between individuals.

Patients should ask their surgeon where the incision will be placed and what type of scar can reasonably be expected.

## Can Rhinoplasty Improve a Crooked Nose?

Yes, rhinoplasty can sometimes improve a crooked or asymmetric nose.

However, crooked noses can involve multiple structural components, including the nasal bones, septum and cartilage.

Correction can therefore be more complex than simply changing the external appearance.

A detailed examination is necessary to determine whether rhinoplasty, [septoplasty](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty) or septorhinoplasty is appropriate.

## Can Rhinoplasty Correct a Deviated Septum?

Rhinoplasty itself and [septoplasty](/costs/India/ENT/Septoplasty) are different procedures.

If a deviated septum is contributing to nasal obstruction, septoplasty may be performed.

When external nasal deformity and internal septal deviation coexist, the procedures may be combined as a septorhinoplasty.

The appropriate approach depends on the patient's anatomy and symptoms.

## Can Rhinoplasty Be Done After a Nose Injury?

Yes.

Trauma can change the shape and internal structure of the nose.

Depending on the timing and severity of the injury, treatment may involve fracture management, septal correction, rhinoplasty or reconstructive surgery.

A specialist assessment is particularly important when an injury has caused both cosmetic deformity and difficulty breathing.

## Can Rhinoplasty Be Repeated?

Revision rhinoplasty is possible, but it is usually more complex than primary rhinoplasty.

Previous surgery may have removed cartilage, altered nasal support, created scar tissue, changed the anatomy or reduced available graft material.

Additional cartilage from the ear or rib may sometimes be needed.

Because the nose continues to heal and change after surgery, surgeons commonly recommend waiting until sufficient healing has occurred before considering revision surgery. Mayo Clinic notes that when a second operation is required, patients may need to wait at least a year.

Revision cases are quoted after records review.

## Rhinoplasty Results: What Is Realistic?

Rhinoplasty can produce meaningful changes, but it cannot guarantee perfection.

The result is influenced by bone structure, cartilage strength, skin thickness, healing response, existing asymmetry, surgical technique, previous surgery and postoperative care.

Even small structural changes can have a noticeable effect on facial appearance.

The objective should be a well-planned improvement that is appropriate for the individual's anatomy rather than an expectation of absolute symmetry or perfection.

## Is Rhinoplasty Safe?

Rhinoplasty is routinely performed by appropriately trained surgeons, but it remains surgery with potential complications.

Safety depends on appropriate patient selection, an experienced surgical team, proper preoperative evaluation, suitable anaesthesia, an accredited or appropriately equipped facility, careful surgical planning, postoperative monitoring and timely management of complications.

Patients should discuss their individual risk profile rather than relying on generalized statements about safety.

## When Should You Contact the Hospital After Rhinoplasty?

Patients should follow the emergency instructions given by their surgical team.

Unexpected or concerning symptoms such as significant bleeding, severe pain, fever, breathing difficulty, visual symptoms or rapidly worsening swelling should be reported promptly.

Do not wait for a routine follow-up appointment if you develop symptoms that your surgical team has identified as requiring urgent attention.

These symptoms belong in a **local emergency department**, not in a WhatsApp message.

It can when breathing difficulty is caused by structural abnormalities that are appropriately treated during surgery.

Rhinoplasty focuses on reshaping or reconstructing the nose. Septorhinoplasty combines nasal reshaping with correction of the nasal septum.

### Is open rhinoplasty better than closed rhinoplasty?

Neither is universally better. The appropriate approach depends on the patient's anatomy and the surgical changes required.

### Will there be a visible scar?

Closed rhinoplasty has no external incision. Open rhinoplasty involves a small columellar incision that generally heals as a discreet scar.
