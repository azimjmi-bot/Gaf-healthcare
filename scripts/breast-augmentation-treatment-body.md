## What Is Breast Augmentation?

Breast augmentation is a surgical procedure designed to increase breast volume and improve breast contour. The medical term is **augmentation mammoplasty**.

The most common approach involves placing an implant inside a surgically created pocket within the breast area. Depending on the patient's anatomy and surgical plan, the implant may be positioned beneath the breast tissue, beneath the pectoral muscle or in another appropriate tissue plane.

Another option is **fat-transfer breast augmentation**, in which fat is removed from another area of the body, processed and injected into the breasts. Fat transfer may provide a more modest increase in volume and is not suitable for everyone.

Breast augmentation is different from a **breast lift**.

A breast lift, or mastopexy, primarily raises and reshapes breast tissue that has become droopy. It does not necessarily create a significant increase in breast volume. Named lift lists sit on [Breast Lift in India](/treatments/breast-lift-in-india).

If a woman has both loss of volume and significant breast drooping, the surgeon may discuss **breast augmentation with mastopexy**. Combined work is quoted after records review.

## Why Do Women Choose Breast Augmentation?

Patients choose breast augmentation for different reasons, and there is no single definition of an ideal breast shape.

Common reasons include:

- Naturally small or underdeveloped breasts
- Loss of breast volume after pregnancy
- Changes following breastfeeding
- Loss of volume following significant weight loss
- Differences in breast size or shape
- Developmental breast asymmetry
- Desire for increased breast fullness
- Desire to improve body proportions
- Reconstruction or correction following certain breast conditions — reconstruction after mastectomy sits on [Breast Reconstruction Surgery in India](/treatments/breast-reconstruction-in-india)

The Indian Association of Aesthetic Plastic Surgeons describes breast augmentation as an option for women with naturally small breasts, loss of volume following weight loss or pregnancy, and noticeable breast asymmetry.

The most important consideration is that the desired result should be discussed realistically with the surgeon. Breast augmentation can increase volume and alter contour, but it cannot guarantee a particular appearance.

## Breast Implant Types

### Silicone Breast Implants

Silicone gel implants contain a cohesive silicone gel inside an outer shell.

They are widely used for breast augmentation and are available in different shapes, profiles and sizes.

Modern cohesive silicone implants are designed to maintain their shape while providing a breast-like feel. However, the exact feel and appearance depend on the implant, the amount of natural breast tissue covering it, implant placement and individual healing.

The FDA notes that breast implants are not lifetime devices and that patients should understand the possibility of additional surgery over time.

### Saline Breast Implants

Saline implants contain sterile saline solution.

If a saline implant ruptures, the saline is generally absorbed by the body and the breast usually loses volume as the implant deflates. A change in breast size or contour may make rupture clinically apparent.

### Round Implants

Round implants can provide increased fullness, particularly in the upper portion of the breast.

They are available in different diameters and profiles, allowing the surgeon to select an implant based on chest width, existing breast tissue and the desired degree of projection.

### Anatomical or Teardrop-Shaped Implants

Anatomical implants are designed with a more tapered shape.

The choice between round and anatomical implants depends on individual anatomy, the surgeon's assessment, implant characteristics and the desired appearance.

The implant's shape alone does not determine whether the result will look natural. Soft-tissue coverage, implant size, placement and surgical technique are also important.

## How Is Breast Implant Size Selected?

Choosing the implant size is one of the most important parts of breast augmentation planning.

A common mistake is to select an implant based solely on a desired cup size.

Cup sizes are not standardized between manufacturers and bra brands. A specific implant volume can also produce very different results in two women with different chest widths, breast tissue and skin elasticity.

Your surgeon may assess chest width, existing breast volume, breast-base diameter, skin quality, breast position, nipple position, breast asymmetry, rib-cage shape, amount of natural tissue coverage, desired projection, lifestyle and physical activity, and whether a breast lift is also required.

The goal should be a medically appropriate implant that fits your anatomy and your desired aesthetic outcome.

## Breast Implant Placement

Breast implants can be placed in different tissue planes.

### Subglandular Placement

The implant is placed behind the breast tissue and above the pectoral muscle.

This approach may be suitable for selected patients with sufficient natural breast tissue.

### Submuscular Placement

The implant is positioned beneath the pectoral muscle.

This can provide additional soft-tissue coverage in patients with limited natural breast tissue, although it can also have specific effects on movement and implant appearance.

### Dual-Plane Placement

A dual-plane technique combines elements of submuscular and subglandular positioning.

The precise technique is selected according to the patient's anatomy, degree of tissue coverage, breast shape and surgical objectives.

The Indian Association of Aesthetic Plastic Surgeons notes that implant placement may be subglandular, submuscular or subfascial depending on available tissue and individual anatomy.

## Breast Augmentation Incisions

The surgeon needs an incision through which the implant can be inserted.

### Inframammary Incision

The incision is made in the natural fold beneath the breast.

This approach provides direct access to the breast pocket and is commonly used.

### Periareolar Incision

The incision is placed around the edge of the areola.

Its suitability depends on the size and anatomy of the areola and other individual factors.

### Transaxillary Incision

The incision is made in the armpit area.

This approach avoids an incision directly on the breast but requires the surgeon to work through a different access route.

The choice of incision depends on anatomy, implant type, surgical technique and surgeon experience.

## Who Is a Good Candidate for Breast Augmentation?

A suitable candidate generally has realistic expectations and is medically appropriate for elective surgery.

Breast augmentation may be considered if you are unhappy with naturally small breasts, have lost breast volume after pregnancy or weight loss, have breast asymmetry, want greater breast fullness, are at a stable weight, understand the potential benefits and risks, are willing to attend follow-up appointments, and understand that implants may require future surgery.

## Who May Not Be Ready for Breast Augmentation?

Breast augmentation may need to be postponed or reconsidered if you are pregnant, are breastfeeding, have an active infection, have an untreated breast abnormality, have certain uncontrolled medical conditions, smoke and are not prepared to stop around surgery, have unrealistic expectations, are undergoing another major medical treatment, or have not yet reached an appropriate stage of physical development.

A history of breast cancer, previous breast surgery, radiation therapy or significant breast abnormalities requires individual assessment.

If there is a personal or strong family history of breast cancer, discuss this with your surgeon before surgery. Additional breast evaluation may be recommended.

## How Breast Augmentation Surgery Is Performed

The anaesthesia team administers the planned form of anaesthesia and monitors you throughout the procedure.

Light movement is generally encouraged after surgery to reduce prolonged inactivity, but strenuous exercise should be avoided during early healing.

Your surgeon may restrict heavy lifting, chest exercises, running, high-impact activities, swimming and extensive arm movements.

The return to exercise should be gradual.

## Breast Augmentation Risks and Complications

Breast augmentation is surgery and carries risks.

Potential complications include infection, bleeding, hematoma, seroma, changes in nipple or breast sensation, persistent pain, visible or unfavorable scarring, implant malposition, breast asymmetry, rippling or wrinkling, implant rupture or leakage, capsular contracture, need for revision surgery and implant removal.

ASPS and the FDA both emphasize that patients should understand these risks before deciding to undergo implant surgery.

A complication does not necessarily mean that the original surgery was performed incorrectly. Some complications can occur even when surgery is performed appropriately.

## What Is Capsular Contracture?

After an implant is placed, the body naturally forms scar tissue around it.

This scar capsule is expected.

In **capsular contracture**, the capsule becomes abnormally tight or firm and can alter the appearance or feel of the breast.

Possible symptoms include increasing firmness, tightness, distortion, discomfort or pain, and change in breast shape.

Treatment depends on the severity and may involve observation, medication in selected circumstances or revision surgery. Revision lists are quoted after review; there is no separate GAF implant-revision sheet.

## What Happens if a Breast Implant Ruptures?

Implant rupture means the outer shell of the implant has developed a tear or defect.

With saline implants, rupture generally causes the implant to deflate as the saline is absorbed.

Silicone implant rupture can be more difficult to detect because the silicone gel may remain within the surrounding capsule. Some ruptures may therefore be clinically silent.

For this reason, long-term monitoring is important.

The FDA recommends ultrasound or MRI surveillance for asymptomatic patients with silicone implants beginning approximately **5–6 years after implantation and every 2–3 years thereafter**, although individual recommendations should be discussed with the treating clinician.

## Breast Implant-Associated Cancer: What Patients Should Know

Patients should also understand the uncommon cancers that have been associated with breast implants.

One is **breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL)**, a rare type of lymphoma associated particularly with certain textured implants.

The FDA and ASPS also discuss reports of other very rare cancers involving the capsule around breast implants, including breast implant-associated squamous cell carcinoma.

These conditions are uncommon, but they are important enough to discuss before surgery.

Seek medical evaluation if you develop persistent swelling, a new breast lump, unexplained pain, significant changes in breast shape or other unusual breast symptoms.

## Breast Implant Illness

Some patients report systemic symptoms after receiving breast implants, sometimes referred to collectively as **breast implant illness (BII)**.

Reported symptoms can include fatigue, cognitive complaints, muscle or joint pain and skin symptoms.

The relationship between these symptoms and breast implants remains an area of ongoing medical discussion and research. ASPS recognizes these patient-reported symptoms while emphasizing that patients with concerns should discuss them with an appropriately qualified clinician.

## Do Breast Implants Increase the Risk of Breast Cancer?

Breast implants do not eliminate the possibility of developing breast cancer.

Patients should continue age- and risk-appropriate breast cancer screening after augmentation.

It is important to tell the mammography facility that you have breast implants. Special imaging techniques may be used to improve visualization of breast tissue.

Breast implant evaluation and breast cancer screening are not the same thing.

The oncology pathway sits on [Breast Cancer Treatment in India](/treatments/breast-cancer-treatment-in-india) when cancer treatment, not cosmetic volume, is the product.

## Can You Breastfeed After Breast Augmentation?

Many women are able to breastfeed after breast augmentation, but breastfeeding outcomes can vary.

Factors that may influence breastfeeding include surgical technique, incision location, changes to the nipple or areola, existing breast anatomy, individual milk production, and previous pregnancy and breastfeeding history.

Breast augmentation should therefore not be presented as guaranteeing either normal or impaired breastfeeding.

If future pregnancy and breastfeeding are important to you, discuss this with your surgeon before surgery.

## Pregnancy After Breast Augmentation

Breast augmentation does not prevent pregnancy.

However, pregnancy can change the breast.

Changes in breast size, skin elasticity, breast tissue and weight can affect the appearance of augmented breasts after pregnancy.

Some women may subsequently choose revision surgery or a [breast lift](/treatments/breast-lift-in-india) if significant changes occur.

## Breast Augmentation and Breast Lift: What Is the Difference?

**Breast augmentation** primarily adds volume.

**Breast lift** primarily raises and reshapes the breast.

If the breasts have significant sagging, simply inserting a large implant may not provide the desired result.

In such cases, the surgeon may recommend **augmentation mastopexy**, which combines an implant with a breast lift.

The Indian Association of Aesthetic Plastic Surgeons specifically notes that severe drooping cannot always be corrected with augmentation alone.

## Warning Signs After Breast Augmentation

Contact your surgeon promptly, and seek urgent care in a **local emergency department** if you experience sudden or rapidly increasing swelling, significant bleeding, fever, increasing redness, severe or worsening pain, pus or abnormal wound discharge, implant exposure, sudden breast asymmetry, new persistent breast swelling, significant change in breast shape, a new breast or armpit lump, sudden shortness of breath, or marked swelling of one calf.

The NHS advises patients to contact their treating clinic when unexpected pain, unusual swelling, burning sensations or skin-color changes occur. Do not wait for a WhatsApp reply if those symptoms develop.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Breast Augmentation in India

### Is breast augmentation safe?

Breast augmentation can be performed safely in appropriately selected patients, but it is still major surgery and has potential complications. These include infection, bleeding, capsular contracture, implant rupture, changes in sensation, pain and the possibility of revision surgery.

### What is the average cost of breast augmentation in India?

Named GAF partner planning is **$3,000–$6,500**, typically 1–3 nights. A personalized quotation is necessary because implant choice and additional procedures can substantially change the cost.

### Which implant is better: silicone or saline?

Neither is universally better for every patient. Silicone and saline implants have different characteristics, benefits and potential complications. The appropriate choice depends on your anatomy, goals and the surgeon's assessment.

### Do breast implants look natural?

They can produce a natural-looking result, but the appearance depends on implant size, shape, profile, placement, existing breast tissue, skin quality and surgical technique.

### How long do breast implants last?

Breast implants are not lifetime devices. Some patients may have implants for many years without major problems, while others may require earlier revision because of rupture, capsular contracture, malposition or other changes.

### Is breast augmentation painful?

Some discomfort, tightness and soreness are expected after surgery. Pain generally decreases during the first several days, but swelling and tenderness can continue longer.

### How soon can I return to work?

Patients with desk-based work may return relatively quickly, but the appropriate timing depends on recovery. Physically demanding work usually requires more time. Follow your surgeon's individual restrictions.

### Can breast implants rupture?

Yes. Implant rupture is a recognized complication. Saline rupture often causes visible deflation, while silicone rupture can sometimes be silent and may require ultrasound or MRI for detection.

### Do I need MRI after breast augmentation?

Not every patient needs an MRI immediately after surgery. For asymptomatic patients with silicone implants, FDA recommendations call for ultrasound or MRI beginning approximately 5–6 years after implantation and then every 2–3 years. Your doctor may recommend different imaging if symptoms or concerns arise.

### Can I have a mammogram after breast augmentation?

Yes. Breast cancer screening can still be performed after breast augmentation. Tell the imaging facility that you have breast implants because additional views or specific techniques may be required.

### Can I breastfeed after breast augmentation?

Many women can breastfeed after augmentation, but breastfeeding ability varies. Discuss your future pregnancy and breastfeeding plans with your surgeon before choosing the surgical technique.

### Can breast augmentation correct uneven breasts?

It can improve breast asymmetry, although perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed. The surgeon may use different implant sizes or techniques depending on the difference between the breasts.

### Can breast augmentation be combined with a breast lift?

Yes. When a patient has both loss of volume and significant drooping, augmentation may be combined with mastopexy and quoted after review.

### How long does breast augmentation surgery take?

The procedure commonly takes around 1–2 hours, although the duration varies with the technique and whether other procedures are performed.

### Is breast augmentation permanent?

The increase in breast volume can be long-lasting, but implants themselves are not lifetime devices. Pregnancy, ageing, weight changes and implant complications can alter the appearance over time.

### Is breast augmentation available for international patients in India?

Yes. International patients should arrange sufficient time for consultation, surgery, recovery and postoperative review before returning home.

### Which city in India is best for breast augmentation?

There is no single best city. Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai and Hyderabad are live GAF catalog cities. Choose a named plastic surgeon who already plans the implant, plane and incision you need.
