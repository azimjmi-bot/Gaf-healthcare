## Quick Answer: IVF Treatment in India

**What is IVF?**
In vitro fertilisation: eggs are retrieved, fertilised in the laboratory, and an embryo may be transferred to the uterus.

**Who may be considered?**
Selected couples or individuals with tubal disease, unexplained infertility after work-up, endometriosis-related infertility, selected male-factor indications, or after unsuccessful simpler treatments when reserve and uterine findings support a cycle.

**Is IVF the same as IUI or ICSI?**
No. IUI places prepared sperm in the uterus. ICSI injects a single sperm into each mature egg inside an IVF cycle. IVF is the laboratory fertilisation pathway; ICSI is a fertilisation technique used when indicated.

**What does IVF cost in India (planning range)?**
GAF planning for IVF Treatment is $2,500–$5,500. Medications, ICSI, freeze-all, FET, donor work and PGT change the episode. Use the Infertility & IVF cost hub for planning detail.

**Does this page publish success rates?**
No. Clinic success rates depend on age, diagnosis, laboratory practice and reporting definitions. GAF does not invent or republish unverified percentages on this page.

**Which hospitals are linked here?**
Catalog fertility centres including Bloom IVF, Nova IVF campuses and Neelkanth Infertility & IVF Hospital, plus the Infertility & IVF hospitals specialty list.

**What records should international patients send first?**
AMH and antral follicle count where relevant, semen analysis, uterine/tubal imaging, infectious-disease screening and prior IUI/IVF cycle summaries.

## What Is IVF Treatment?

IVF retrieves mature eggs after controlled ovarian stimulation, fertilises them with sperm in the embryology laboratory, cultures embryos, and transfers an embryo when the plan allows—or freezes suitable embryos for a later [frozen embryo transfer (FET)](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Frozen-Embryo-Transfer-FET).

Conventional IVF places eggs and sperm together for fertilisation. When sperm quality, prior fertilisation failure or laboratory indications support micro-injection, the same cycle may use [ICSI](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/ICSI-Treatment). Genetic testing of embryos such as [PGT-A](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/PGT-A-Testing) is a separate add-on when clinically indicated—not a silent upgrade inside every package.

## Who May Need IVF?

A fertility specialist may consider IVF when simpler treatments are unsuitable or have failed, and when ovarian reserve, uterine receptivity and legal gamete rules support a cycle. Typical clinical contexts include:

- Tubal disease or blocked tubes where natural fertilisation or IUI is unlikely to help
- Unexplained infertility after appropriate evaluation
- Endometriosis-related infertility when surgery alone is not enough
- Selected male-factor infertility, often with ICSI or surgical sperm retrieval such as [Micro-TESE](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Micro-TESE)
- Need for donor eggs or donor sperm under applicable Indian rules — see [Donor Egg IVF](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Donor-Egg-IVF) and [Donor Sperm IVF](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Donor-Sperm-IVF)
- Fertility preservation before gonadotoxic treatment — see [Fertility Preservation](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Fertility-Preservation) and [Egg Freezing](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Egg-Freezing)

IUI remains a different product for selected unexplained or mild male-factor cases. PCOS-related anovulation may begin with [ovulation induction](/costs/India/Infertility-IVF/Ovulation-Induction) or timed intercourse before IVF is considered. This page does not rank those sequences; the named specialist does after records review.

## How an IVF Cycle Works

1. **Record review** — AMH, follicle count, semen analysis, uterine cavity assessment, infectious screening and prior cycle charts.
2. **Named plan** — stimulation protocol, whether conventional IVF or ICSI is expected, fresh versus freeze-all, and whether PGT or donor pathways are in scope.
3. **Stimulation and monitoring** — gonadotropins with ultrasound and hormone checks; trigger timing is individual.
4. **Egg retrieval** — usually day-care under sedation; overnight stay is uncommon unless OHSS risk or anaesthesia concern requires it.
5. **Laboratory fertilisation and culture** — conventional IVF or ICSI; cleavage-stage or blastocyst culture as planned.
6. **Transfer or freeze-all** — fresh transfer when appropriate, or cryopreservation with later FET.
7. **Luteal support and follow-up** — medicines, pregnancy testing and obstetric handover when pregnancy occurs.

## International Patient Coordination

1. Send records before booking non-refundable travel.
2. Confirm the exact campus, embryologist pathway, estimate assumptions and whether donor or PGT counselling is required under current rules.
3. Keep flights and lodging flexible until stimulation start and retrieval windows are realistic for both partners when both must attend.
4. Before departure home, obtain cycle summaries, embryo reports, medicine lists, warning signs for OHSS or post-retrieval complications, and named follow-up contacts.

## Risks and Limits

Material risks include ovarian hyperstimulation, bleeding or infection after retrieval, anaesthetic events, multiple pregnancy if more than one embryo is transferred, cycle cancellation, and unsuccessful fertilisation or implantation. No facilitator can guarantee pregnancy. Legal rules for donor gametes and surrogacy must be confirmed for the intended pathway—do not assume every brochure option is available to every international patient.
