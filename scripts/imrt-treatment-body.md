## Quick Answer: IMRT in India

**What is IMRT?**
A highly planned form of external beam radiation therapy in which radiation intensity is varied across multiple beams to conform the dose more closely to the target.

**What does IMRT treat?**
It can be used for many cancers, including head and neck, prostate, brain, spine, breast, pelvic and other tumours where precise dose distribution is important.

**Is IMRT painful?**
The radiation delivery itself is generally painless. Some patients may experience discomfort from remaining in position or from immobilization devices.

**How long does IMRT take?**
Treatment courses vary widely. Conventional fractionated courses may involve weekday treatments over several weeks, while some cancers can be treated using shorter schedules.

**How long is each session?**
The actual radiation delivery is usually brief, although positioning and image verification add time.

**Does IMRT require hospitalization?**
Usually not. Most external-beam IMRT is delivered on an outpatient basis.

**What machine is used?**
A medical linear accelerator (LINAC) is commonly used.

**Is IMRT the same as IGRT?**
No. IMRT describes how radiation dose is modulated; IGRT refers to imaging used to verify patient or tumour positioning during treatment. They are often used together.

**Is VMAT a type of IMRT?**
VMAT is an arc-based form of intensity-modulated radiation delivery in which the machine rotates around the patient while modulating dose delivery.

**How much does IMRT cost in India?**

**Can international patients get IMRT in India?**
Yes. India has AERB-licensed radiotherapy centres, including centres offering modern IMRT lists.

## What Is IMRT?

IMRT is a type of [external beam radiotherapy](/treatments/external-beam-radiotherapy-in-india).

Instead of delivering the same intensity across a field, IMRT varies intensity in different portions of the beam. Multiple beams enter from different directions so the team can build a customized dose cloud.

The objective is to deliver the prescribed dose to the target while reducing unnecessary dose to nearby organs. That matters when a tumour sits next to salivary glands, spinal cord, rectum, bladder, bowel, heart or lung.

IMRT does not make normal-tissue dose zero. Margins and beam physics still spill some dose. The honest claim is **better dose distribution**, not zero radiation to healthy tissue.

## How Does IMRT Work?

Planning starts with a CT simulation. MRI or PET/CT may be fused when they change the target. The radiation oncologist draws the target and organs at risk. A physicist and dosimetrist then calculate beam shapes and intensity patterns.

Delivery uses a linac with a multileaf collimator. The patient stays on the couch while the machine delivers the approved plan from one or more directions. Image guidance may confirm position before beam-on.

IMRT is not a stronger radiation. It is a more sophisticated way to plan and deliver the same prescribed dose.

## Why IMRT Differs From Conventional Radiation

Traditional fields may use relatively uniform intensity. IMRT adds modulation so dose can wrap an irregular target and spare selected organs.

That distinction matters when the tumour shape is complex, several structures have different constraints, previous radiation has already used some tolerance, or long-term toxicity reduction is a planning goal.

A simpler 3D-CRT list can still be honest when modulation does not change the plan.

## Who May Be a Candidate?

A radiation oncologist weighs cancer type, stage, location, shape, nearby organs, previous surgery or radiation, systemic therapy, intent, dose, fractions, anatomy and the ability to hold the treatment position.

IMRT is especially useful when the target is irregular or close to organs that need sparing. It is not automatically better for every patient. The named plan decides.

## IMRT Treatment Process

![Unlabeled four-step pathway from records to CT simulation, inverse planning and daily linac treatment](/uploads/treatments/im-steps.webp)

1. **Consultation.** Pathology, imaging and previous dose are reviewed. International patients can send records before travel.
2. **Intent.** Cure, adjuvant, neoadjuvant, local control, re-irradiation or symptom relief is written down.
3. **CT simulation.** The patient is positioned as for treatment. Head-and-neck lists often use a thermoplastic mask.
4. **Image fusion.** MRI or PET is registered when it changes the target.
5. **Contouring.** Gross tumour, microscopic-risk volumes, margins and organs at risk are drawn. A sophisticated machine cannot rescue a poorly drawn target.
6. **Inverse planning.** The software searches beam arrangements and intensity maps. The radiation oncologist approves coverage and constraints.
7. **Physics QA.** The plan and machine are checked before the first fraction. AERB regulates Indian radiotherapy facilities, equipment, shielding and qualified staff.
8. **First fraction.** Positioning and image verification precede beam-on.
9. **Daily treatments.** Many conventional lists run Monday to Friday. Fraction count still varies.
10. **On-treatment review.** Fatigue, weight, skin, swallowing, bowel, bladder and nutrition are watched. Anatomy change can force a replan.

GAF IMRT planning is typically **4–7 weeks of fractions**. That is a planning frame, not a prescription. Ask for the exact fraction count.

The beam itself is usually brief. Positioning and verification take longer. The gantry does not normally touch the patient. Beam-on is generally painless.

## Side Effects

IMRT improves dose shaping. It does not erase side effects. Early effects appear during or soon after the course. Late effects can appear months or years later.

**General.** Fatigue, skin redness or darkening, reduced appetite and local discomfort.

**Head and neck.** Dry mouth, thick saliva, mouth sores, swallowing difficulty, taste change and eating problems. Dental review before treatment matters.

**Prostate and pelvis.** Urinary frequency or urgency, bowel change, rectal irritation, diarrhoea and, in some patients, sexual change.

**Breast.** Skin change, tenderness, swelling and later texture change. Planning may limit heart and lung.

**Brain and spine.** Fatigue, localized hair loss, headache, swelling or selected neurological symptoms.

Inability to swallow fluids, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, uncontrolled bleeding, high fever or fainting belongs in a **local emergency department**.

## Benefits and Limits

Potential advantages are more conformal dose, better sparing of selected organs, usefulness near critical structures and, for some sites, lower specific toxicities such as dry mouth.

Limits remain: planning is more complex, QA is mandatory, normal tissue still receives dose, side effects still occur, cost can rise, and IMRT does not guarantee cure. Technology does not replace contouring, physics or follow-up.

## IMRT vs 3D-CRT, IGRT, VMAT, SBRT and Protons

| Feature | 3D-CRT | IMRT |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | More uniform within each field | Can vary across the field |
| Planning | Lower complexity | Inverse planning |
| Dose shaping | Conformal | More highly modulated |
| QA | Required | Especially important |

**VMAT** is an arc-based intensity-modulated delivery. The gantry rotates while intensity, dose rate and leaves change. There is no live GAF VMAT treatment page.

**SBRT** is a few high-dose stereotactic fractions. It can use intensity modulation. It is not automatically competing with a several-week IMRT list.

**Protons** use a different particle and Bragg peak. They are not automatically better. The neighbouring sheet is [proton beam therapy](/costs/India/Radiation-Oncology/Proton-Beam-Therapy).

## Preparation, Nutrition, Fertility and Follow-Up

Preparation is site-specific: bladder filling, bowel routine, dental care, skin products and fasting instructions come from the treating team, not from another patient's list.

Nutrition matters on a multi-week course, especially head-and-neck lists. A dietitian helps when swallowing, mouth sores, diarrhoea or weight loss appear.

Radiation-related hair loss is usually limited to the treated field. Fertility risk depends on dose to reproductive organs. Discuss preservation **before** the first fraction when children are planned.

Re-irradiation needs the previous plan. IMRT can sometimes shape around previously treated structures. That remains a specialist decision.

Follow-up watches response, late effects, weight, organ function and imaging. Some effects appear late. IMRT is a delivery technique, not a guarantee of cure.

Ask why IMRT rather than 3D-CRT, whether IGRT is used, how many fractions, which organs are constrained, whether chemotherapy or brachytherapy is added, whether the bunker is AERB-licensed, and what the written package includes.
